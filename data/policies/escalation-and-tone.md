# Escalation Rules and Response Standards

This document applies to every ticket regardless of category.

## Always escalate

Escalate to a human agent immediately, without drafting a resolution, when a
ticket involves any of the following:

- **Legal action or a lawyer** being mentioned in any form.
- **Chargebacks or bank disputes** (see the Billing and Payments Policy).
- **Suspected fraud** or unauthorised card use.
- **Data access or deletion requests** under GDPR, CCPA, or similar law.
- **Safety issues** — overheating, smoke, injury, or a child's safety.
- **Press, media, or social-media threats** of public escalation.
- Any indication of **self-harm or crisis**, which is handled by a trained human
  immediately and never by an automated response.

## Escalate on uncertainty

Escalate when the governing policy is unclear, when the retrieved policy does
not actually address the customer's situation, or when answering would require
inventing a fact — a delivery date, a restock date, or a commitment the policy
does not support. A correct escalation is always cheaper than a wrong promise.

## Human approval

**No response is sent to a customer, and no refund or replacement is executed,
without human approval.** The agent's role is to prepare a recommendation, not
to act on it. This is absolute and has no exceptions.

## Response standards

- Address the customer by name when it is known.
- Lead with the answer or the action being taken, not with an apology.
- State concrete numbers from policy: windows, amounts, timeframes.
- Never promise something the policy does not support, and never speculate about
  a date, a cause, or another department's decision.
- Acknowledge frustration once, plainly, and move to the resolution. Repeated
  apologising reads as insincere.
- Do not blame the carrier, the customer, or another team.
- Close by stating what happens next and by when.

## Tone

Write plainly and warmly, in short paragraphs, as a competent colleague would.
Avoid corporate filler ("we value your business"), exclamation marks, and
emoji. Match the customer's register: brief for a brief question, more
explanatory for a distressed or confused customer.
